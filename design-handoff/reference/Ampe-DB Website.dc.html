What is Ampe?

Ampe is a traditional Ghanaian game played by two people who jump, clap and reveal a leg position in a tightly coordinated interaction. Its patterns make it a valuable setting for studying paired movement rather than isolated single-person actions.

Ampe-DB turns short, carefully organized segments of natural gameplay into a resource for computer vision and human interaction research, while preserving the cultural context of the game. It is deliberately compact rather than large-scale.

04.1

Dataset overview

Ampe-DB captures two-person interactions from Ampe, a Ghanaian game involving coordinated jumping, clapping and a leg-position reveal. The benchmark treats each segmented clip as a paired movement configuration rather than a solo action.

The dataset is deliberately compact rather than large-scale. Its contribution is the combination of culturally grounded interaction, explicit participant/pair metadata, aligned RGB and pose representations, and leakage-aware generalization protocols.

RGB video and audio

Canonical media are MP4 clips with synchronized embedded audio. Frame rate and codec vary across canonical media and are documented in metadata.

Important

Do not describe V1 as a uniform 60 FPS dataset. Read frame rate and codec from the release metadata for every clip.

Normalized pose CSV

The canonical pose representation is long-form, with up to 33 MediaPipe landmarks per player per frame:

frame, player_id, landmark_index, x_norm, y_norm, visibility
  • x_norm and y_norm are body-normalized coordinates, not simple [0, 1] image coordinates.
  • Normalization uses the hip midpoint as origin and a shoulder-to-hip torso scale.
  • No rotation or reflection normalization is applied.
  • Missing detections and incomplete player-frame groups are handled explicitly during model preparation using presence indicators.

Skeleton exports

Skeleton CSV and JSON files preserve pixel-space landmark geometry for visualization and reconstruction. Skeleton MP4 files are derivative visualizations for inspection, debugging, demonstration and publication figures — not a separate ground-truth modality.

Pair identity is essential to the V1 evaluation design. The primary generalization benchmark holds out participant pairs as pairs, while individual participants may still appear with different partners in training for a given fold.

04.8

Recording conditions

Capture design

  • Single fixed frontal camera viewpoint.
  • Two players visible together within the frame.
  • Source recordings captured natural Ampe play and were later segmented into canonical interactions.
  • Embedded audio retains clap, step and vocal cues from the source recording.

Camera equipment

Recording documentation identifies Blackmagic and Sony A7 III cameras in the source capture workflow. Rely on per-file media metadata rather than assuming one uniform camera profile for every canonical clip.

05.2

Evaluation protocols

A generalization ladder: each step moves the test set further from what the model saw in training.

  1. 01

    Duplicate-safe fixed

    Reference benchmark

    Locked split of 444 train / 97 validation / 96 test records. Exact-duplicate audiovisual groups stay within one partition, removing duplicate leakage.

    444 train 97 validation 96 test
  2. 02

    Pair-held-out

    Primary benchmark

    Four folds. Test participant pairs do not appear as pairs in training for that fold; individual participants may still be seen with different partners. The primary benchmark for unseen pair combinations.

  3. 03

    Participant-held-out diagnostic

    Diagnostic

    One participant is absent from training and validation and appears only in diagnostic test interactions. Six participants are eligible for four-class evaluation and eight for binary evaluation. A complementary check on unseen-participant transfer.

Terminology

Say pair-held-out for the primary cross-pair protocol and participant-held-out diagnostic for the unseen-participant analysis. Do not call the latter participant-disjoint: the held-out participant can interact at test time with partners seen during training.

The protocols distinguish fitting familiar players from learning movement structure that transfers to unseen pairings and participants.

05.4

Raw video refinement

Source gameplay recordings are refined into short canonical clips that preserve the complete Ampe interaction, with consistent record identifiers for annotation, pose extraction, skeleton rendering and benchmarking.

  1. 01ScreenCheck source recordings for usable two-player visibility and stable capture.
  2. 02SegmentCut continuous play into short clips around the jump–clap–leg reveal cycle.
  3. 03LabelAssign the canonical movement token.
  4. 04TrimRetain enough temporal context around the interaction.
  5. 05VerifyReview visually; keep the C### identifier across all downstream representations.

Publication principle

Refinement preserves the natural movement dynamics of the source interaction. This documentation describes the published V1 pipeline, not earlier exploratory segmentation claims that are not reflected in the frozen release.

05.5

Pose & skeleton extraction

V1 uses 33-landmark MediaPipe pose outputs for each represented player and stores the canonical normalized representation as long-form CSV rows.

Processing

  1. 01Process canonical RGB clips frame by frame.
  2. 02Maintain two-person track identity through the clip using the extraction/tracking pipeline and release mappings.
  3. 03Store normalized x/y coordinates and visibility values in the canonical pose CSV.
  4. 04Export corresponding pixel-coordinate skeleton CSV and JSON for rendering.
  5. 05Generate rendered skeleton MP4 videos for qualitative inspection.
Identity caution

Do not define participant identity only by “leftmost person = Player A” across the dataset. Participant and pair IDs in the release mapping files are authoritative.

Missing detections

Pose estimation is imperfect and some frame-player groups are incomplete. Baseline V1 model preparation includes presence indicators rather than assuming all 66 player-landmark positions are observed in every frame.

05.6

Quality control & validation

Release-level QC goals

  • 1:1 identifier coverage across RGB, normalized pose, skeleton CSV, skeleton JSON and skeleton video.
  • Readable RGB media with synchronized embedded audio.
  • Pose/skeleton frame, player and landmark schema consistency.
  • Participant/pair mappings consistent with canonical clip IDs.
  • Exact-duplicate groups documented and kept within fixed benchmark partitions.
  • Split definitions and label files validated against the canonical manifest.

Skeleton review

Rendered skeleton motion is compared with the original RGB footage. Structural checks validate frame counts and CSV/JSON consistency, while manual review identifies identity swapping, limb misalignment, tracking drift or localized anatomy errors. Repairs are localized and revalidated.

05.7

Known limitations

Documented openly so results can be interpreted within the scope of the release.

Scale
637 canonical clips from 15 participants and 11 unordered pairs is focused rather than large-scale.
Exact duplicates
557 unique complete-file RGB/audio hashes across 637 records. Provenance is transparent and controlled in the fixed split, but duplicate content remains a source-corpus limitation.
2D front view
Pose provides no depth and should not be treated as 3D biomechanics or marker-based motion capture.
Pose estimation noise
Fast jumps, overlap and low-confidence detections can produce missing or imperfect landmarks.
Non-uniform media
Most clips are 30 FPS, with smaller 24 FPS and 60 FPS subsets; H.264 and HEVC both occur.
Participant-held-out coverage
Only participants with sufficient class coverage are eligible: 6 for the four-class task and 8 for the binary diagnostic.
Fusion stability
Simple late fusion is not uniformly superior to pose, and some RGB-heavy alpha selections are unstable in diagnostics.
Cultural scope
The corpus does not represent all Ampe variants or African movement broadly.
Skeleton visualizations
Derivative QC artifacts; they do not certify perfect anatomical reconstruction in every frame.
